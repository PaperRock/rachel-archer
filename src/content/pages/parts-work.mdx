<Section>
  <Prose>
    <p class="lead">If you have ever felt pulled in two different directions at once — like a side of you that wants to take a bold leap and another side that desperately wants to stay safe — you have already experienced the foundation of Internal Family Systems (IFS).</p>

    <p>Developed by Dr. Richard Schwartz in the 1980s, IFS is an evidence-based approach to psychotherapy built on a simple, compassionate premise: the human mind is naturally multi-faceted. Rather than having a single, rigid personality, we are made up of dynamic inner “parts,” each with its own perspective, feelings, and intentions.</p>
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section alt>
  <Split>
    <div slot="left">
      <h2>What are “parts”?</h2>
    </div>
    <Prose slot="right">
      <p>In IFS, your inner world is viewed much like a family. Every part of you developed to help you navigate life, adapt to challenges, and keep you safe. Even behaviors or thoughts that feel frustrating or counterproductive usually stem from a part trying to protect you in the best way it knows how.</p>

      <h3>Protective parts</h3>
      <p>These are the inner voices that keep daily life structured or react quickly during stress. Some work proactively (like perfectionism, hyper-vigilance, or people-pleasing) to prevent pain, while others work reactively (like sudden emotional checking-out, busyness, or strong impulses) to soothe pain when it arises.</p>

      <h3>Vulnerable parts</h3>
      <p>These parts hold painful memories, hurt, or fears from earlier experiences. When protective parts are active, they are often trying to guard these sensitive inner places from being overwhelmed again.</p>
    </Prose>
  </Split>
</Section>

<Section>
  <Split>
    <div slot="left">
      <h2>The core concept: the Self</h2>
    </div>
    <Prose slot="right">
      <p>At the heart of IFS is the understanding that underneath all your protective and vulnerable parts lies your true Self. The Self is not a part, but your core essence — characterized by natural qualities such as calm, clarity, compassion, confidence, and curiosity.</p>

      <p>In IFS therapy, the goal is not to eliminate, fight, or “fix” any part of you. Instead, therapy helps you step into Self-leadership, allowing you to build a compassionate, understanding relationship with your own internal system.</p>
    </Prose>
  </Split>
</Section>

<Section alt>
  <Split>
    <div slot="left">
      <h2>Key benefits of IFS therapy</h2>
    </div>
    <Prose slot="right">
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Self-compassion without self-blame:</strong> By viewing difficult habits or thoughts as protective parts rather than personal flaws, shame decreases, making room for true healing.</li>
        <li><strong>Emotional resilience:</strong> Learning to meet intense emotions with curiosity rather than overwhelm allows you to navigate life’s stressors with steady groundedness.</li>
        <li><strong>Deep healing at the root:</strong> IFS addresses the core wounds carried by vulnerable parts, allowing old burdens to gently clear rather than simply managing symptoms.</li>
        <li><strong>Internal harmony:</strong> Instead of battling conflicting thoughts, parts work brings your inner system into alignment, fostering genuine peace and self-trust.</li>
      </ul>

      <p>Engaging in parts work offers a transformative path back to yourself — one where every aspect of your story is met with dignity, understanding, and care.</p>
    </Prose>
  </Split>
